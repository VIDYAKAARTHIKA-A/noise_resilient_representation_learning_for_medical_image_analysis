Learning Reliable Medical Representations from Noisy Labels

An undergraduate honours project asking whether noise-aware sample weighting, kNN/GMM label-cleanliness estimates and relabeling can improve classification of dermoscopy images when training labels are corrupted and the eight diagnostic classes are heavily imbalanced. Research visualization only: not clinically validated and not for diagnosis.

Every number is labelled: measured from your run outputs, calculated from measured data or code, pending not available yet.

Dataset

ISIC 2019, 8 classes. Split is a stratified 70/15/15 (train_test_split, seed 42); noise touches the training split only.

Test counts are measured (sum 3,800). Noisy-train counts come from your detection logs (sum 17,731 = 70% of 25,331).

The Eight Diagnostic Classes

Three malignant, one pre-cancerous and four benign categories. Short educational descriptions, not clinical guidance. Missing a rare malignant class such as SCC is costly, which is why this project looks at per-class results and not just accuracy.

The Problem: Imbalance and Label Noise

The noise policy is uniform_global: exactly int(p·N) random training samples get a label drawn uniformly from the other classes. Large classes lose labels to small ones, and small classes are flooded with wrong ones.

Fraction of each given label that is actually correct from logs

With 40% noise only about 9% of samples labelled DF and 10% labelled VASC are truly DF or VASC. This is a property of the injection policy, not of the model.

Accuracy alone hides this: on the test set NV is 51% of images (1,931 of 3,800) while DF is under 1% (36). A model can score well while missing DF and SCC entirely, so macro metrics and per-class recall are reported.

Methodology

The implemented pipeline, end to end: data and noise, SimCLR pretraining, supervised baselines, and the proposed noise-resilient training.

What do the metrics mean?

Accuracy, precision, recall, F1

Accuracy = correct / all. Precision = TP/(TP+FP); recall = TP/(TP+FN); F1 = 2PR/(P+R). Averages here are macro (unweighted mean over the 8 classes, sklearn zero_division=0) unless labelled weighted. Balanced accuracy equals macro-recall in this code, so it is shown once.
